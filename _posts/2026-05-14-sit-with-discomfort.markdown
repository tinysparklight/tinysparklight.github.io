Something that I have been thinking about a lot is being able to *sit with discomfort*.

A recent event acted as a forcing function. Certain responsibilities vanished from my plate in an instant and that has afforded me some time to contemplate and introspect. I am realizing that a major part of my daily discontent and sorrow comes from resisting what is, in other words, finding ways to avoid facing discomfort of any kind - uncertainty, disappointment, not being good enough, not being efficient or productive enough, not having enough time, the burden of taking important decisions, or anything else one might label as a 'problem'. Resisting never makes the problem go away, the problem still remains, probably bigger, at least in my head.

The word *problem* has a negative connotation. People with best intentions have tried to encourage me to see things in a different light. Why don't you turn it into something positive? See it as an opportunity, call it a puzzle, a challenge, something exciting. I am sorry. Honestly, this approach has almost never helped me. This train of thought makes me feel like a charlatan, and a very ineffective one at that. It's time to try something different.

Let's go one step back. 

Why do I label something as a problem? When something, external (a circumstance or person) or internal (a thought), triggers something unpleasant in me, it's a problem. That "something unpleasant" is the feeling of discomfort that I was referring to earlier. What if I just allowed myself to sit and witness this feeling? Why do that, you might ask? Because many people whom I respect have pointed out that there might be great power in that, and I have a sneaky suspicion that they are onto something. Hear me out.

As I get better at being okay with discomfort, I build evidence that I can handle anything that comes my way, because inside, _I_ am ultimately going to be okay. I don't need to go around begging or praying for situations or people to be a certain way to constantly protect myself from pain and discomfort, because I am able to sit with that unpleasantness.

Not resisting allows me to accept reality as is, so that I might respond from a place of greater clarity. This is important because being able to *sit with discomfort*, without resisting, doesn't mean that we just coast through life, witnessing from the sidelines. It helps us see things as they are, without adding layers of false perception from the baggage we carry, and then if necessary, take appropriate action, or let go, if things are really outside one's circle of influence. Not resisting frees up mental resources that can instead be employed to discern how one should respond.

I started reading the book *The Road Less Traveled* recently. The author M. Scott Peck starts with the foundational premise "Life is a series of problems". Sit with that for a minute. I did and I can agree with that statement drawing from my personal experience. So if life is series of problems, trying to resist problems is trying to resist life, so I am living while trying to resist living. That seems pretty counterproductive. The only way through life is by navigating and solving the problems one's faced with but the first step is to be able to be okay with suffering because problems are never going to feel great.

The ability of being able to *sit with discomfort* has another wonderful side effect. It allows experiences to pass through us. That means that we feel what we need to feel, without resisting, sitting in the seat of consciousness. We don't get dragged into the scene and start identifying with it. So it's able to pass through us and doesn't become baggage that we have to carry for the rest of our lives. Paraphrasing Michael Singer "It's not due to the _nature_ of the stuff - pleasant or unpleasant - inside, that it's still sitting inside of you, it's because you are holding on to it that it's still in there."

It is possible that when I *sit with discomfort*, I might realize that something is not even a *problem* or the *problem* actually lies elsewhere. This kind of insight only comes from a place of clarity and acceptance, when I take the time to *sit with discomfort* and stop resisting.

There is a trap here that I became conscious of today morning. I was playing with my toddler and in my head, unfortunately, going through my seemingly infinite to-do list, feeling the weight of human finitude very acutely. Then probably because of all the reading and contemplating I have been doing, I caught myself in this negative loop and decided to just witness the storm of thoughts and feelings inside. Soon there was a feeling of letting go, not trying to grasp at some ray of hope, or some way out of the prison of doing doing doing. There was a moment in which I suddenly felt great, but then a thought came along, "wait, why am I feeling happy, I haven't really solved any of my problems". Followed by, "oh, this sitting with discomfort is great and all, but it doesn't solve my problems, it _doesn't make me any happier_", and I felt horrible again. This is the trap, the wrong expectation. Non-resistance is not supposed to make me happier. That wasn't the goal. The goal is to be able accept reality, the fact that there are problems, limitations. In this case, it seems wiser to acknowledge the limitations of being human. We have many constraints - time, capabilities, capacity, energy. Instead of being a debilitating thought, this should feel like a liberation from unrealistic expectations. Now I can channel and dedicate my time and energy more wisely by prioritizing things and people that really matter. For instance, playing with my toddler with more presence.

*Sit with discomfort*, let it pass through. Accept reality first and then respond appropriately. Take responsibility and use the freedom and power that accompanies it.

Here's a poem by Rumi that I chanced upon (in Aika's *fieldnotes* from Substack titled ["four poems I keep coming back to"](https://theoxfordpsych.substack.com?utm_source=navbar&utm_medium=web) - I absolutely adore everything she shares and she feels like a soul sister, though she doesn't know me, obviously). Also who doesn't love Rumi?!

> This being human is a guest house.
> Every morning a new arrival.
>
> A joy, a depression, a meanness,
> some momentary awareness comes
> as an unexpected visitor.
>
> Welcome and entertain them all!
> Even if they’re a crowd of sorrows,
> who violently sweep your house
> empty of its furniture,
> still, treat each guest honorably.
> He may be clearing you out
> for some new delight.
>
> The dark thought, the shame, the malice,
> meet them at the door laughing,
> and invite them in.
>
> Be grateful for whoever comes,
> because each has been sent
> as a guide from beyond.
>
> \- Rumi (13th century)
