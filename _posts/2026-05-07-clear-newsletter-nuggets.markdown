Today I want to share some memorable quotes I read in James Clear's "3-2-1 Thursday" newsletter. 

> The most expensive part of a bad habit is rarely the action itself, but rather the identity it quietly reinforces underneath. The issue isn't skipping once. It's that you practiced being someone who skips.
> Now, that doesn't mean you need to be perfect. Everyone falls off from time to time. But it is good to have a plan for getting back on track quickly. And try to find a small way to practice being the type of person you want to be today.

Clear is asking us to pay close attention to the identity that each of our habits reinforces. This is a generally neglected side-effect of behaviors we exhibit and is true for any habit, good or bad. What this also means is that when we are architecting a new habit for our routine, we should be conscious of the fact that the habit is quietly going to add to or take away something from our identity.

He also recommends for each new habit you are working on adding, have a really tiny version of it hashed out as well which you can implement on days you feel you have no energy or motiviation or willpower or time. The purpose of this is, again, to keep up the fortification of your new identity.

> The disciplined life looks impressive, but the well-designed life lasts longer. Before you try to find more willpower, design your environment and arrange your day so you're more likely to follow through.

Here James Clear is highlighting the importance of architecting your environment to set yourself up for success instead of relying on the mysterious and generally unreliable force of willpower. In his popular book Atomic Habits, he recommends doing this by setting up the supporting systems, right and obvious cues and stacking habits.
