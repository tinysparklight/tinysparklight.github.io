I saw a great YouTube [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iGkVqeFUVNU) by Daniel Pink where he shared lessons from the book Happy Money by Elizabeth Dunn and Michael Norton. It's about how to increase your chances at happiness while spending money, and I am not complaining about the snappy term *happy money* I get to add to my vocabulary, because growing up, we have all heard versions of the incomplete not-helpful philosophy *money is the root of all evil*.

Before your next big (or small) expenditure, ask yourself the following questions:

1. is this going to give me back time or steal my time? (outsource chores, pay for time-saving tools, make time to do things you enjoy and deem important)
2. is this going to add to my life story? (experiences over material things)
3. is this going to bring me closer to someone I care about? (lends to more sustained happiness and deeper relationships)
4. can I make it an occasional treat? (beware the hedonic adaptation)
5. can I pay for it now and consume it later? (anticipation is its own form of happiness)

Rate and score yourself on these 5 questions based on this scale: 0 for disagree, 1 for undecided, 2 for agree. If you score 6 or more, you should probably go for the purchase; less than 4, you need to walk away! Pink provides some really great examples for each of these questions in his video and I am sure that the book has even better ones, so I am leaving out these from my post (fine, yes, I am being lazy).

I have tried out this idea for a few purchases now and have felt it ease the decision making process. See if it helps you too!
