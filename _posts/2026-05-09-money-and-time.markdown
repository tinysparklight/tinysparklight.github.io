We have already heard the adage "Time is money." I heard this a lot while growing up, and generally assumed that it meant that time is as important as money, the importance of money hammered into our young minds constantly in the Indian society. I Googled its meaning today and learned that the original source text of this aphorism was trying to convey the economic concept of opportunity cost - time spent on doing something implies not doing something else that could have instead earned you money.

The adage flipped onto its head is "Money is Time". I came across this idea in a [Substack article by YJ](https://open.substack.com/pub/yewjin/p/the-case-for-less?r=25olb&utm_campaign=post-expanded-share&utm_medium=web) and what he put forth in his article really resonated with me, hence I am recording it below. Also, I am by no means perfect at following the advice that follows, but this is me writing about it to process it and hopefully remember it better in the situations I will need it.

YJ starts by proposing that when it comes to financial freedom, the question we should focus on should be "how do I stop needing so much that work becomes a trap?", instead of the seemingly simpler, but less helpful question of "how do I stop working?".

He recommends incorporating 3 principles in your life to strive towards the goal of financial freedom.

### Avoiding Lifestyle Creep

Here he presents the idea that **money is time**. Every dollar you spend represents the time you spent earning that dollar. 

In my opinion one form of freedom is being able to do what you want with your time. I also include this in my personal definition of success, though obviously that is not the only pre-condition to success. When you spend money on things you don't need, you might have to spend time on work you don't want to do, thereby losing your freedom in some sense.

Having a minimalistic lifestyle in which you spend minimal money on things you don't actually need, and when you do, you think about the *money is time* tradeoff consciously. This does not mean that you deny yourself pleasure in life, but you decide to spend on the basis of your wealth, not income. Not falling for the trap of trends and peer-pressure are also crucial for avoiding lifestyle creep.

YJ recommends

> Question every recurring expense. Periodically reset your baseline by stripping back to less, on purpose, and noticing how little you miss.

### Placing Asymmetric Bets

YJ defines an asymmetric bet as follows

> An asymmetric bet is any decision where the worst case is small and survivable, and the best case is big.

Even when you invest in yourself by learning new skills, you are placing an asymmetric bet on yourself. YJ mentions that each skill you learn widens the gap between what you can earn and what you need to spend, and in that gap lies your financial freedom.

YJ recommends

> Learn skills, build assets, take positions where the downside is small and the upside compounds over time, and be patient.

### Striving For Less

A dollar saved is worth more than a dollar earned. If invested, the saved dollar compounds over time and also, when you have to earn a dollar, you actually need to pay tax on it too.

Falling into the trap of buying more and nicer things when our income increases or wealth accumulates only increases anxiety about the said things since they all require maintenance and upkeep. We have heard platitudes on the lines of *Money is evil*, but some studies have found that more money can indeed make people happier if it's used to gain more control over their own time and energy - if the money is providing security and freedom over how you spend your time, then more of it can actually make you happier. Money is simply a tool, and your attitude towards it and the way you use it makes all the difference.

YJ recommends

> Define your “enough” before the culture defines it for you.
