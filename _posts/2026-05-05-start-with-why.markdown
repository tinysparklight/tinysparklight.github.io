A little bit of housekeeping today. I am going to attempt to pen down my thoughts around what motivated me to start this blog. And recursively, the reason why I want to attempt this is because it will be good to have something to come back to when (note: not 'if', but 'when') I feel lost or start feeling pressure about the publishing frequency or quality of the content (these are not my goals, at least not at the moment). I hope that this post will help me stay grounded and help me keep coming back whenever I feel the need to share anything at all. In the true engineering spirit (hello eng. design docs), I will list below my goals, having shared the non-goals above. So, here we go...

I foresee this blog as an outlet for my overthinking self. All people who know me well have at least once in my life told me 'don't overthink it!' Not sure whether I have been very successful in following their well-meaning advice, but I know that writing down my thoughts helps my very prolific mind process them better. I know this to be true from my journaling experience - whenever I am embroiled in a particularly overwhelming day, journaling helps me articulate the things that I am only 'feeling' so that I can pinpoint to the exact things bothering me.

So you might ask, why not do this privately, in your personal journal? Why thrust your innermost workings on the rest of the world? Well, to remind you, this is not for you to read (unless it's future-me). I did share in my first post that this blog is only for myself, I request and in fact, warn you to get out before it's too late ;) So the reason I want to do this publicly is to build a practice of sharing my thoughts publicly. Over time, due to the way I was raised (no grudges, just a string of observations) and the situations I was faced with, I developed the tendency to keep my thoughts and opinions to myself. So I want to practice "speaking out" more. Even though I ask you, reader, to not read this blog, there's nothing useful here for you, it's still a public piece my inner world. So I am hoping that sharing my thoughts here will help me practice share more and also over time help me become more articulate (fingers crossed!).

Another reason is to create a log of things that stuck with me as I went about my life. Like most other humans on this planet I am a consumer of content. I watch videos, movies, listen to podcasts and books, read books and online articles, observe nature, people, other things around me. I do this mostly to entertain myself but also to inform. I concede that I forget most things, but some things stick with me for a little longer - things I resonate with, things that articulate something that I feel but am unable to put into words, things that I am amazed by, things that I want my future self to remember, things that I want to imbibe in my own life or thinking. I want to create a log all of these things. This is a mighty ambition, but I want to give it a good old try.

Disclaimers:

- I might go back and edit my posts (in fact I most definitely will since I am approaching this so casually right now)

- I will not use any AI to create / edit my content because the goal is not to publish top quality content for others to consume, but to write down thoughts that my mind is creating (not thoughts of the genies, as Kent Beck calls them); I might take AI's help to fact-check certain things (or maybe not! I might be lazy and I don't claim to be always factually correct)

- my posts might have grammmatical or spelling errors since English is not my first language, I am sorry if that annoys you

- I know that I will cringe at my posts later on but isn't that true for any new endeavor? you hope that you get better with time which implicitly means that you will cringe at your early stuff, so it's probably a good thing :)

Alright then, till next time!

P.S. This post is titled 'Start with why' which as you might already know is the title of a book by Simon Sinek. This book has been on my TBR and on my bookshelf for the longest time. I love and agree with the premise (hence this post), and will get around to reading it sometime (soon?) - apologies to all the wonderful books waiting to be read... someday...
